What happens after an EV plugs in

Follow a charging session from driver payment to verified revenue and the costs required to keep the site operating.

Two questions stay together

What creates economic value—and what the energy source, efficiency, equipment life and end-of-life handling mean for the full system.

The payment is only the beginning

A driver connects, selects a session and pays through the supported payment flow. That transaction creates gross revenue, but it does not equal the final project result. Electricity, processing, site costs, maintenance and operations still need to be recorded.

The useful question is not only “How many cars charged?” but “Which revenue and costs were verified for the same period?”

From session to operating record

  1. 01

    Record the session

    The system records energy delivered, time, charger, price and payment status.

  2. 02

    Verify revenue and costs

    Payment records are matched with electricity, processing, site, network, service and maintenance costs.

  3. 03

    Apply the Charge agreement

    The verified Charge revenue, company reserve and written obligation follow the Charge-specific funding and settlement rules.

What makes the explanation credible

  • A documented source for charging-session and payment records.
  • A consistent period for revenue, energy and operating expenses.
  • A clear distinction between gross revenue, project costs and the company obligation.

Public examples are not live financial evidence

A presentation can explain the sequence and required controls. Actual revenue, reserve funding and payments require separate verified records and the project agreement.

Charge uses a fixed company obligation for a UTC calendar month, with continuous accrual and settlement every 4 days. These terms apply only to Charge and still require verified funding and the written agreement.
