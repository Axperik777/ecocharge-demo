AI infrastructure7 min read

AI begins in software. Its capacity depends on physical infrastructure.

Trace the energy, cooling, network, accelerator capacity and operating responsibility behind an AI workload before comparing providers.

Two questions stay together

What creates economic value—and what the energy source, efficiency, equipment life and end-of-life handling mean for the full system.

AI revenue depends on a physical system and paid demand

A model or application can make AI feel like pure software. Behind the interface, every workload still depends on accelerators, electricity, cooling, network access, orchestration and people who operate the facility.

For infrastructure participation, the decision starts with evidenced paid demand and follows the full chain from site and equipment to operating costs, replacement, rights, risks and exit.

CURRENT CONTEXT · CHECKED OCTOBER 11, 2026

What the current infrastructure shift actually shows

Industry roadmaps and energy forecasts explain the scale of the engineering problem. They do not prove that EcoGrid owns a specific system, has available capacity or will deliver a particular commercial result.

ENERGY FORECAST · NOT ECOGRID CAPACITY

Global data-center electricity demand is expected to more than double by 2030

The IEA links AI growth to a rapid increase in data-center electricity demand. This is global market context, not evidence of an EcoGrid site, energy source or available GPU inventory.

Ironwood was introduced as a TPU designed for large-scale inference

Google describes the accelerator together with networking and liquid-cooling requirements. It illustrates why chips, power and facilities must be planned as one system; it does not imply a partnership with EcoGrid.

Trace the participation model behind the infrastructure

  1. 01

    Evidence paid demand

    Separate expected interest from signed or billed workloads, and show how price, contracted volume and utilization create project revenue.

  2. 02

    Map the infrastructure

    Confirm the site, accelerators, memory, interconnect, storage and ownership or procurement responsibilities instead of relying on a familiar chip name.

  3. 03

    Map energy and operations

    Review metering, grid connection, any on-site generation and storage, cooling, backup, monitoring and maintenance. Solar alone does not establish continuous 24/7 supply.

  4. 04

    Confirm economics and rights

    Put capital needs, operating costs, replacement, reporting, decision rights, risks and exit conditions into the participation proposal and agreement.

Questions for AI-infrastructure due diligence

  • Which paid workloads, contracts and billing records support the demand assumptions?
  • Which site, accelerators, memory, interconnect and storage are confirmed, and who owns or procures them?
  • How are energy, cooling, network, monitoring, maintenance, downtime and reserves documented?
  • Which rights, reports, replacement rules, risks and exit conditions are fixed in the participation agreement?

Market momentum is not proof of a project result

This guide explains how to review AI infrastructure participation. It does not confirm EcoGrid assets, paid demand, ownership or a future result. Those facts and the participant’s rights require project evidence, a separate proposal and a written agreement.
