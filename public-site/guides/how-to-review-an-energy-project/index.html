How to review an energy project before deciding

Use one repeatable review to separate the story, operating evidence, written rights and unresolved questions.

Two questions stay together

What creates economic value—and what the energy source, efficiency, equipment life and end-of-life handling mean for the full system.

A clear presentation is the start of a review, not the end

A project can explain its technology, market and purpose convincingly. The next step is to connect each important statement to a document, source, responsible party or explicit assumption.

The goal is not to remove every uncertainty. It is to know which facts are confirmed, which terms are contractual and which outcomes remain variable.

A four-part review

  1. 01

    Business and operations

    Identify the customer, revenue source, operating stage, equipment, site, operator and recurring costs.

  2. 02

    Rights and documents

    Review the legal party, agreement, ownership or operating rights, reporting, fees, settlement and exit terms.

  3. 03

    Evidence and assumptions

    Separate sourced operating data from examples, model inputs, future plans and marketing explanations.

  4. 04

    Risk and decision

    List counterparty, capital, market, equipment, regulatory and execution risks before choosing the next step.

Questions to keep in one place

  • What exactly am I buying, renting or participating in?
  • Which result is fixed by contract and which result can change?
  • What evidence is available now, and what still requires verification?

Education does not replace professional review

The Academy can organize information and questions. It does not verify legal status, guarantee an outcome or replace legal, tax, technical or financial advice.
