Solar for your property or project participation: two different decisions

Separate a property equipment purchase from participation in a Solar project before comparing documents or outcomes.

Two questions stay together

What creates economic value—and what the energy source, efficiency, equipment life and end-of-life handling mean for the full system.

The same word “Solar” can describe two different goals

A property owner may want equipment that changes how the building uses electricity. Another person may want to review participation in an operating Solar project. The equipment, rights, economics and agreements are different.

If you need a system for your own property, continue with the property review below. If you are considering participation in a separate Solar project, review its terms before making a request.

Review Solar participation terms

How the two paths differ

  1. 01

    Equipment for a property

    The review focuses on the address, tariff, installation area, components, permits, installation scope and final price.

  2. 02

    Participation in a project

    The review focuses on the project site, energy buyers, generation assumptions, operating costs, rights, reporting, risks and exit terms.

  3. 03

    Separate proposals

    Each path should produce its own written proposal and documents. One request must not silently activate or replace the other.

Choose by the outcome you need

  • Lowering or reshaping electricity use at your own property points to equipment.
  • Studying a separate energy business points to project participation.
  • Wanting both still requires two scopes, two decisions and separate documents.

Do not carry terms from one path into the other

Equipment warranties and an installed-system quote do not define project participation. Project economics do not guarantee property savings or equipment performance.
