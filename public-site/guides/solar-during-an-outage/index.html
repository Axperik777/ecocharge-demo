Solar during an outage: when a battery is required

Panels, storage, controls and grid isolation play different roles when the utility supply is unavailable.

Two questions stay together

What creates economic value—and what the energy source, efficiency, equipment life and end-of-life handling mean for the full system.

The outage happens after sunset

A rooftop array produced energy during the day, but the outage begins in the evening. Without stored energy there is no daytime generation left to use. Even during daylight, a grid-connected system may shut down unless it is designed to operate safely in backup mode.

Backup is therefore a system-design question, not a feature that comes automatically with every set of panels.

What creates a working backup system

  1. 01

    Choose essential loads

    List the circuits that must keep working and how long they should operate.

  2. 02

    Size power and capacity separately

    Battery power limits what can run at once; usable capacity affects how long those loads can run.

  3. 03

    Design controls and isolation

    Compatible controls and grid-isolation equipment are required to create a safe backup circuit.

Questions for the system proposal

  • Which loads are included and which remain outside the backup panel.
  • Expected operating time under the documented load assumptions.
  • Battery warranty, usable capacity, power limits and operating conditions.

A battery is not unlimited power

Actual backup time changes with load, temperature, battery state, system losses and future solar generation. The written design should state its assumptions.
