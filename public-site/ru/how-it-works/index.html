How EcoGrid works

The sequence is deliberate: electrical work created the foundation; Charge added experience with distributed loads and connections; Solar and storage added generation and peak control; Compute is the next managed demand for energy, cooling and network capacity. Shared engineering can support the system; every project keeps separate economics and agreements.

EcoGrid Charge — EV charging infrastructure

Economic value

Drivers pay for charging sessions. The signed plan defines the company payment obligation, its funding and the four-day settlement.

Environmental responsibility

EVs have no tailpipe emissions while driving. The site still needs transparent data on its electricity mix, utilization, efficiency and equipment life.

EcoGrid Solar — Solar generation and storage

Economic value

Solar plants produce electricity for sale or on-site use. Generation, energy-sale terms and operating costs determine the project result.

Environmental responsibility

Panels generate electricity without burning fuel on site. Materials, service life, repair and end-of-life handling remain part of the assessment.

EcoGrid Compute — Managed GPU capacity and AI infrastructure

Economic value

Businesses can request measured or reserved GPU capacity. A separate participation format, where available, reviews contracted demand, revenue, energy, cooling, network, service, downtime, replacement and exit terms for a defined site.

Environmental responsibility

Solar and storage can support a site, but every location must document its actual electricity mix, grid connection, backup, cooling, efficiency, equipment life, repair and end-of-life handling.

One infrastructure. Three products.

Model, not telemetry

EcoGrid grew from electrical and energy engineering. Charge was the first practical direction: interconnection, distributed-load, peak and energy-cost experience led to Solar and storage. Compute is the next step, adding GPUs, cooling, networking and data-center operations. Shared infrastructure may reduce some capital and operating costs, but revenue, expenses, risks, terms and agreements are calculated separately for each project.

EcoGrid AI

EcoGrid AI is our shared operating intelligence across every project. It compares generation, demand, tariffs and equipment condition to help allocate energy where it delivers more value and reduce avoidable costs. Operators set the limits and approve operating decisions.

The physical energy path depends on the site, interconnection, tariff, load and agreement. Compute does not promise 24/7 solar-only operation; grid supply and storage are part of project planning.

Shared foundation — separate economics

Projects may share sites, interconnection, transformers, storage, monitoring and an operating team. But Charge, Solar and Compute have different revenue sources, expenses, risks, offers and agreements.

A separate EcoGrid Charge rule

42% of verified Charge revenue is applied first to the fixed company payment obligations. If that amount is insufficient, an eligible company reserve covers the shortfall; any uncovered amount remains recorded as company payable and is never silently written off. This rule does not present the remainder as a client or company ownership share.

After agreement activation, the selected Charge plan creates a fixed company payment obligation of 13–16% per UTC calendar month, accrued continuously by time. The obligation is not reduced by weak station revenue, but its fulfilment depends on the company and its reserve; it is not a guarantee of capital repayment.

Who pays for the result

EV drivers

Pay for completed Charge sessions.

Solar customers

Buy equipment, design and installation or discuss separate participation in an energy project.

Compute customers

Pay for confirmed GPU capacity and an agreed service level; any participation result is calculated only under a separate project model.
