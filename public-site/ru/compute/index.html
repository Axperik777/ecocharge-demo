WHY COMPUTE EXISTS

AI infrastructure is more than processors.

High-density GPU systems require confirmed power, heat removal, network capacity, backup and continuous operation. Access to computing therefore depends on the whole site—not only on the accelerator model.

HOW THE SYSTEM WORKS

From site power to a billed GPU workload.

  1. 01
    Power and backup

    The site plan confirms grid capacity, generation, storage and backup requirements.

  2. 02
    Cooling and network

    Heat removal, connectivity and service access determine which systems can operate safely.

  3. 03
    GPU configuration

    The proposal records the hardware class, memory, region, available capacity and operating limits.

  4. 04
    Customer workload

    Capacity is reserved or billed by measured usage under a separate service agreement.

The energy mix is confirmed for each site.

Solar and storage can help manage load where technically feasible, but they do not mean continuous operation from sunlight alone. Grid connection, backup, cooling and uptime are designed separately. Charge, Solar and Compute retain separate contracts and economics.

PROJECT ECONOMICS

Project result depends on paid workloads and actual costs.

Billed GPU hours or reserved capacity−Energy · cooling · network · service−Downtime · replacement · reserves=Variable project result

Utilization risk

Available hardware does not create revenue until customers reserve or use the capacity.

Technology risk

New processors can change price, demand and the useful economic life of existing hardware.

Operating risk

Power limits, cooling, network incidents, maintenance and downtime can change service quality and cost.

Contract and capital risk

The agreement must define billing, responsibility, data handling and exit. Participation can produce a negative result and put capital at risk.
